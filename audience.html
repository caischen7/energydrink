TOTAL US DEMAND — TODAY AND 2030

The chart above is convenience-store sell-through, which is roughly 60% of the category. These two scale the same nine audiences to the whole US market — $26.9B in 2025 — and then project it forward. Hover either ring for the dollar figure behind a slice.

WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM

Convenience shares are measured from the PDI transaction data. Multi-outlet shares are measured from Mintel MULO brand sales — mapping each brand to its audience. The two are blended on Mintel's own channel weights (convenience $16.1B, supermarket $3.6B, all other $7.2B). The gap between the first two columns is the finding: women are 11.8% of convenience sales but 28.2% of multi-outlet, which is why the c-store-only view understates them.

WHY THE THREE CHARTS DISAGREE

The same nine audiences appear three times on this page with different numbers. That is not an inconsistency — two separate things are changing, and it is worth keeping them apart.

1 · Channel chart 1 → chart 2

Nothing about consumers changes here. The first chart counts only convenience stores; the second counts the whole market. Audiences that sell through grocery, mass and club grow; audiences that live in the c-store cooler shrink.

    2 · Time chart 2 → chart 3

    Now the market itself moves, from $26.9B to $38.6B. Shares here are projections of measured trends, not a re-measurement. Note that every audience except Calorie-cutters still grows in dollars — a falling share of a market that grows 43% is not a shrinking business.

      THE SHORT VERSION

      Young adults fall in both steps — slightly on channel (67.6% → 64.9%, because Red Bull and Monster are distributed everywhere, so their audience looks similar in any channel) and sharply over time (64.9% → 56.5%). They are not losing customers; they are being diluted as the category recruits people who were never energy-drink buyers.

      Gym & fitness falls in both steps too, and this one is a real decline rather than dilution: performance brands are convenience-heavy, and the two with grocery distribution are shrinking outright (Bang -8%, Reign -22%). The energy that used to go into "pre-workout in a can" has migrated to the wellness framing instead.

      Women is the only audience that rises in both steps, and it rises a lot: +6.6pp from counting the right channels, then +9.9pp more from trend. Roughly two fifths of that increase is a measurement fix, not a forecast — those sales are already happening today and the c-store-only view simply could not see them.

      REASONING, AUDIENCE BY AUDIENCE

      HOW THE 2030 SPLIT WAS BUILT — AND WHAT WOULD BREAK IT

      The total is not my forecast: it is Mintel's central projection of $38.6B for 2030, whose own 90% confidence band runs from $30.0B to $47.2B. What I projected is the split, by taking each audience's measured share drift — from PDI 2021-2025 and from MULO 2025-2026 — and damping it 15% a year so trends flatten rather than run to absurdity.

      Two figures are set by hand rather than trended, and both are stated rather than hidden: Gamers & creators and Health-conscious adults are held at floors of 1.6% and 0.9%, because both sell mainly through e-commerce and natural grocery, which neither measured channel covers. Trending them from blind data would have projected them to near zero, which would be a measurement artefact rather than a forecast.

      The main risk is the women's line. It carries the whole story — 18.4% to 28.3% — and rests on a trend that has run for six years without slowing. If Celsius and Alani Nu are approaching saturation rather than still compounding, that line lands nearer 22% and the young-adult decline is correspondingly gentler. Worth noting that every audience except Calorie-cutters grows in absolute dollars: this is a category broadening, not one segment beating another.
