WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM

Convenience shares are measured from the PDI transaction data. Multi-outlet shares are measured from Mintel MULO brand sales — mapping each brand to its audience. The two are blended on Mintel's own channel weights (convenience $16.1B, supermarket $3.6B, all other $7.2B). The gap between the first two columns is the finding: women are 11.8% of convenience sales but 28.2% of multi-outlet, which is why the c-store-only view understates them.

REASONING, AUDIENCE BY AUDIENCE

HOW THE 2030 SPLIT WAS BUILT — AND WHAT WOULD BREAK IT

The total is not my forecast: it is Mintel's central projection of $38.6B for 2030, whose own 90% confidence band runs from $30.0B to $47.2B. What I projected is the split, by taking each audience's measured share drift — from PDI 2021-2025 and from MULO 2025-2026 — and damping it 15% a year so trends flatten rather than run to absurdity.

Two figures are set by hand rather than trended, and both are stated rather than hidden: Gamers & creators and Health-conscious adults are held at floors of 1.6% and 0.9%, because both sell mainly through e-commerce and natural grocery, which neither measured channel covers. Trending them from blind data would have projected them to near zero, which would be a measurement artefact rather than a forecast.

The main risk is the women's line. It carries the whole story — 18.4% to 28.3% — and rests on a trend that has run for six years without slowing. If Celsius and Alani Nu are approaching saturation rather than still compounding, that line lands nearer 22% and the young-adult decline is correspondingly gentler. Worth noting that every audience except Calorie-cutters grows in absolute dollars: this is a category broadening, not one segment beating another.
