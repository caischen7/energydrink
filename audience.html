CHANNEL MAKEUP — WHERE THE $26.9B IS BOUGHT

Measured Mintel data, not a model. This is the section that justifies every caveat elsewhere on this site: convenience is the only channel the transaction data can see, and it is both the slowest-growing of the three and shrinking as a share of the market.

    2019 vs 2025 — WHICH CHANNEL ACTUALLY GREW

    The number that matters: convenience has lost 8.8 points of share since 2019 — 68.7% down to 59.9% — while e-commerce, club and foodservice gained 6.0 points and grew 28% in 2025 alone. Any read of this category built only on convenience data is measuring the part that is losing ground.         WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM

        Three independent sources measure this category and they do not agree, so the table below shows all three rather than one. Euromonitor Passport is the anchor, because it is the only one that covers every channel including e-commerce and reports the full brand list. PDI sees convenience only; Mintel MULO sees grocery, mass and club but top brands only. That last limitation matters: an earlier version of this page used MULO to stand in for all non-convenience sales and put women at 18.4%. Passport measures 12.1%. MULO's top-brands-only universe over-weights Celsius and Alani Nu, so that estimate was too high and has been revised down.

        WHY THE THREE CHARTS DISAGREE

        The same nine audiences appear three times on this page with different numbers. That is not an inconsistency — two separate things are changing, and it is worth keeping them apart.

        1 · Channel chart 1 → chart 2

        Nothing about consumers changes here. The first chart counts only convenience stores; the second counts every channel, anchored on Passport. Value brands and performance brands are convenience-heavy, so they shrink; the nationally-distributed giants take a larger share of the whole market than of the c-store cooler alone.

          2 · Time chart 2 → chart 3

          Now the market itself moves, from $26.9B to $38.6B. Shares here are projections of measured trends, not a re-measurement. Note that every audience except Shift workers & military still grows in dollars — a falling share of a market that grows 43% is not a shrinking business.

            THE SHORT VERSION

            Young adults rise on channel (67.6% → 73.0%) and fall over time (73.0% → 64.1%). The rise is the correction: Red Bull and Monster hold 34.5% and 32.2% of all-channel retail value, more than convenience alone shows. The fall is real dilution as the category recruits people who were never energy-drink buyers.

            Gym & fitness falls in both steps, and this one is a real decline: performance brands are convenience-heavy, and Passport shows the segment peaking at 9.3% in 2021 and drifting to 7.3% by 2025. The energy that used to go into "pre-workout in a can" has migrated to the wellness framing instead.

            Women barely moves on channel (11.8% → 13.2%) but climbs hard over time (13.2% → 22.4%). Passport's own series is the evidence: this audience was 0% of the category in 2020 and is 12.1% five years later. Almost none of the increase is a measurement artefact — it is the fastest real shift in the category.

            REASONING, AUDIENCE BY AUDIENCE

            HOW THE 2030 SPLIT WAS BUILT — AND WHAT WOULD BREAK IT

            The total is not my forecast: it is Mintel's central projection of $38.6B for 2030, whose own 90% confidence band runs from $30.0B to $47.2B. What I projected is the split, by taking each audience's measured share drift — from Euromonitor Passport 2021-2025, all channels — and damping it 15% a year so trends flatten rather than run to absurdity.

            Two figures are set by hand rather than trended, and both are stated rather than hidden: Gamers & creators and Health-conscious adults are held at floors of 1.6% and 0.9%, because both sell mainly through e-commerce and natural grocery, which neither measured channel covers. Trending them from blind data would have projected them to near zero, which would be a measurement artefact rather than a forecast.

            The main risk is still the women's line — 13.2% to 22.4% — and the risk is sharper than an earlier version of this page admitted. It claimed the trend had not slowed. It has: the segment added 4.1 points of share in 2023, 2.9 in 2024 and 1.0 in 2025. The projection implies 1.84 points a year, which is 1.8× the most recent measured year. If the deceleration is real rather than a one-year wobble, this lands nearer 17% than 22.4% and the young-adult decline is correspondingly gentler. Note this projection now runs on Passport's ten-year all-channel series rather than a single year of MULO, which is a materially firmer base. Worth noting that every audience except Calorie-cutters grows in absolute dollars: this is a category broadening, not one segment beating another.
