Type a flavor. The chart below pulls every energy-drink SKU carrying that word and plots its convenience-channel revenue month by month, against US search interest for the same word — so you can see whether people searching leads to people buying.

    SALES vs SEARCH

    Both lines are indexed to their own peak, because they are measured in different units — dollars against a 0–100 search index. Indexing compares shape, which is the only honest comparison between the two.

    —

    DOES SEARCH LEAD SALES?

    Correlation is computed on month-over-month change, not on the levels you see plotted. Two series that both drift upward correlate at 0.9 no matter what they measure; differencing removes that and leaves the real question — when search moves, does spending move with it?

    THE CONTROL — DOES THE WHOLE CATEGORY DO THIS?

    Before trusting any single flavor, check the category. If total energy-drink revenue already moves with generic "energy drink" search, then a flavor that appears to track its own search term may just be riding the same seasonal wave twice — and would show a correlation whether or not anyone searched for that flavor. This is the number every result below has to beat.

    EVERY TERM AT ONCE

    The same test run across every term with a collected search series. One term clearing p < 0.05 means little; what matters is how many clear it out of how many tested, against how many would clear by chance alone. The table applies a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across the whole family.
