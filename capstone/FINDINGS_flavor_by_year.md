# Flavor by year — what the PDI panel says

Generated by `capstone/scripts/analyze_flavor_year.py` from
`data/bq/derived/price_panel.csv` (PDI convenience panel, 2019-01 → 2025-12,
84 months, 14 flavor families). Page: `flavors.html`.

## Headline: three quarters of the category's "growth" is PDI signing stores

| | 2019 | 2025 | growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw category units | 144,662,102 | 434,891,016 | **3.01×** |
| Active stores in panel | 8,526 | 19,064 | **2.24×** |
| Units per active store | 16,968 | 22,813 | **1.34×** |

The decomposition is exact: 2.24 × 1.34 = 3.01. Splitting it on logs,
`log(2.24) / log(3.01)` = **73% of the compound growth is coverage**, not
consumption. Every raw annual total in this dataset is therefore
uninterpretable on its own, which is why unit **share** leads every ranking
here and per-store velocity is reported as an index.

rewards proliferation; the per-SKU column is the control.

## The finding that changes the strategic read

A first-versus-last comparison names four winners. **All four have already
peaked:**

| Family | 2019 → 2025 | Peak | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berry | +7.99pp | 2024, 20.82% | −5% off peak |
| Watermelon | +4.56pp | **2021**, 7.35% | **−27% off peak** |
| Tropical | +1.90pp | 2023, 9.09% | **−30% off peak** |
| Apple & pear | +1.17pp | 2023, 2.33% | −11% off peak |

Watermelon is the clearest trap: it reads as the second-biggest gainer of the
window while having fallen for four of the last five years. Only **Novelty &
branded** (13.70%) and **Grape** (4.11%) are at their peak in 2025.

Falling hardest: Punch & mixed fruit −5.20pp (velocity index 44), Original
−4.10pp (still the largest family), Peach & stone fruit −2.50pp (velocity
index 32 — down two thirds per store).

## Highest rated — a much weaker answer

**PDI carries no rating field.** It is point-of-sale: units, revenue,
transactions, stores. Nothing in it can rank quality, in any year.

The only rating data in the repo is a single Amazon snapshot captured
**2026-05-13** — one day, one retailer, 51 products after variety packs are
excluded. It has no time dimension and cannot be split by year.

Bayesian-adjusted per the brief, `(nR + mC)/(n+m)` with C = 4.549 and
m = 1,023 (median review count):

| Family | Adjusted | Products | Reviews |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical | 4.670 | 5 | 18,273 |
| Punch & mixed fruit | 4.657 | 4 | 8,912 |
| Berry | 4.621 | 13 | 26,919 |
| … | | | |
| Peach & stone fruit | 4.547 | **1** | **2** |
| Cola & soda | 4.448 | 2 | 428 |

The whole spread is **0.22 of a star**, narrower than the sampling error on
groups this size. Peach & stone fruit is one product with two reviews and a raw
3.5 that the prior drags almost entirely back to the mean — the adjustment
working correctly, and a sign the sample cannot support a ranking.

**Do not present this as "highest rated."** The defensible version is
Workstream 1: manual multi-retailer capture with a recorded protocol.

## Limitations

1. **Convenience only**, ~8.6% sample of that channel. E-commerce, club and
   natural grocery are invisible — which is where wellness and DTC-native
   flavors live.
2. **Units, not dollars.** A family skewing to 16oz would outrank one skewing
   to 8.4oz on revenue alone. Price per unit is in the table.
3. **Taxonomy is ordered first-match.** "Grape Slush" is Sour & candy, "Mango
   Peach" is Tropical, cherry is Berry. See `capstone/registry/` — four of
   these are open decisions (D-01…D-04) and any of them would move these
   numbers.
4. **"Novelty & branded" is 13.70% and is not a flavor direction.** It is the
   bucket for invented names. Real as a positioning trend, useless as a
   flavor brief.
5. **"Unspecified" is excluded** from every ranking — it is the blank-FLAVOR
   bucket, and letting it place would report a data gap as a finding.
6. **`price_panel.csv` has no recorded query** (decision D-07). Everything here
   inherits an unaudited category filter.
