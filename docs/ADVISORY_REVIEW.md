# Advisory panel review — Market-Intelligence Workstream

Five independent reviewers (simulated advisory personas: a seed-stage CPG VC, a growth-stage beverage investor/operator, an exited functional-beverage founder, a consumer-analytics technical founder, and a former national-retail category buyer) reviewed [`WORKSTREAM.md`](WORKSTREAM.md) independently; a synthesis pass merged their feedback. Generated 2026-07-07.

## Executive summary

All five panelists converge on the same verdict: the engineering and methodology hygiene (fractional SoV attribution, the Eminem-style entertainment noise filter, partial-month exclusion, tested incremental scrapers) is better than most paid social-listening products, but the tool currently measures point-in-time attention levels in channels (Amazon, grocery, social) that carry a minority of US energy-drink volume, while the category is decided by velocity and distribution in convenience stores it cannot see. The unanimous highest-value change is converting the scrapers into a scheduled longitudinal panel whose dated snapshots yield free velocity proxies (review-count deltas, Amazon BSR movement, price/promo cadence) — every reviewer independently named some form of this as the killer feature. Before that lands, two low-effort fixes are mandatory for credibility: normalize prices to $/oz-$/serving (the current price-quality scatter mixes single cans, 12-packs, and $49.50 powder tubs and "would not survive five minutes of scrutiny") and stamp panels with sample sizes plus a channel-bias disclosure (brands rated on 8 reviews currently render with the same authority as brands with hundreds). The consensus positioning: useful today for messaging, flavor, and positioning work on Bogus Banana; not yet decision-grade for pricing, channel, or investment calls until velocity, nutrition attributes, and a c-store proxy exist.

**1. Normalize all retail prices to $/fl-oz and $/serving (pack size + format aware)**  
Named by all five reviewers and flagged as the single highest-leverage fix by the technical founder: brand_summary.csv's amazon_avg_price_usd mixes single cans, 12-packs (Monster $30.74), and powder tubs (AdvoCare $49.50), making the price-x-quality scatter and any price-tier white-space read actively misleading. Pack-size data is already scraped; this is parsing plus a rebuild of the price panels, and it unlocks the first real pricing decision for Bogus Banana ($/oz ladder vs Celsius/Monster/Red Bull).

**2. Add sample-size (n=) and confidence badges to every panel, plus a channel-bias coverage note**  
Three reviewers independently flagged that thin cells (AdvoCare: 1 product / 8 reviews; Instagram fixed at 15 posts/brand — a scrape-depth artifact presented as engagement share) render with the same visual authority as robust ones, and that the no-c-store sample-frame skew is undisclosed. This is trust infrastructure: grey out cells below a minimum n, stamp freshness, and add a one-line coverage disclaimer. It prevents the dashboard from steering a bad decision and is the difference between a buyer/investor nodding versus picking it apart.

**3. Scheduled snapshot layer producing velocity proxies (review-count deltas, Amazon BSR, price/promo cadence, 'bought recently' badges, availability)**  
The unanimous killer feature — all five reviewers named a version of it. Everything today is a point-in-time level, and levels tell you who is big while rates of change tell you who is winning (Celsius-2021 vs Bang-2023). Store dated per-run snapshots instead of merging into one current-state table, then compute week-over-week deltas per brand x retailer and add a velocity tab. Review-count velocity + BSR trajectory is the closest free analog to Circana unit-velocity data; it also fixes the single-crawl survivorship bias in the current YouTube-reconstructed momentum. Ship minimally first (weekly snapshots + deltas on fields already scraped), then extend.

**4. Capture nutrition/claims from retailer PDPs and build the functional positioning map (caffeine x sugar x $/oz, electrolyte/flavor tags)**  
All five reviewers: for a banana-electrolyte concept the entire white-space argument is attribute-level, and this axis is currently parked on the roadmap. Scrape caffeine mg, sugar g, electrolytes, calories, sweetener system, and claims into a per-SKU attribute table, then plot the category and drop the Bogus Banana concept on the map. This directly answers the core question — is moderate-caffeine/electrolyte/fruit-flavor an empty cell, or does Celsius/Alani/Liquid I.V. already own it — and it crosses naturally with the crash/sugar complaint sentiment already mined.

**5. Distribution-breadth panel from the nine retailer scrapers already built (SKU count per brand per retailer, share of assortment, availability)**  
Distribution is the strongest predictor of CPG share growth and the first question a buyer asks ('what's your ACV?'). The scrapers already collect the rows — this is pure aggregation into brand_summary.csv and one new panel, and it also closes the noted gap that Walmart/retailer/TikTok/ads scrapers aren't folded into the brand table yet. Output is directly actionable for a launch brand: 'Alani has 14 SKUs at Target but 2 at Publix — Southeast grocery is open.' Becomes a trended ACV proxy once the snapshot layer lands.

**6. Google Trends demand series via pytrends for brand + flavor/need-state terms**  
The only free source of true multi-year, survivorship-free demand-side time series. It cross-validates the YouTube mention-share momentum (currently a single-source claim built from one crawl), and directly tests the banana/electrolyte flavor thesis against real search demand ('electrolyte energy drink', 'banana energy drink') instead of comment word counts. Costs an afternoon and fits the free-only constraint.

**7. Convenience-channel proxy: scrape Instacart/Gopuff/DoorDash c-store storefronts (7-Eleven, Circle K, Casey's) for assortment, single-can pricing, availability**  
The biggest structural blind spot, named by all five: c-store/gas is ~55-60% of US energy-drink volume, single-can impulse pricing has its own architecture ($1.99-$3.49), and the current Amazon/grocery frame systematically over-weights DTC/lifestyle brands (G Fuel, Ghost, Alani) and under-weights vault incumbents (NOS, Reign) — exactly the wrong bias for a brand planning retail entry. Delivery-app storefronts are the tractable path (direct c-store sites are harder). Higher effort than the other items, but without it every 'where' answer is structurally wrong.

**8. Calibration + back-test methodology page (SoV vs Statista/earnings market shares; momentum vs Celsius-rise / Bang-collapse / Alani-acquisition timelines)**  
Two complementary trust mechanisms the investors converged on: (a) plot the tool's platform SoV against published dollar shares already in data/market/ and show per-brand residuals — divergence itself becomes the product ('Brand X is 3x its dollar share in social voice'); (b) back-test the momentum signal against three dated known outcomes and publish the hit/miss. A momentum signal nobody has validated is an opinion; this is the cheapest way to make anyone weight the tool's forward calls against syndicated data.

## Where the panel disagreed

- Effort on the c-store proxy: the seed VC rates it high effort (direct 7-Eleven/Circle K scraping), while the growth investor, technical founder, and category buyer rate it medium by going through Instacart/Gopuff/DoorDash storefronts instead. Resolution: take the delivery-app path first (medium), treat direct c-store sites as a later hardening step.
- Effort on the velocity/snapshot layer: the growth investor calls the full version (BSR, OOS, promo cadence, a velocity tab) high effort; the beverage founder argues a minimal version (weekly snapshots + review-count/rank deltas) is medium and should ship now. Resolution: ship the minimal version immediately, iterate — the value is in starting the clock on dated snapshots.
- Whether existing single-crawl momentum is salvageable: the technical founder argues the 60-month YouTube-reconstructed momentum inherits search-ranking survivorship bias and cannot be trusted retroactively; the seed VC treats it as usable if cross-validated (Google Trends, back-test). Resolution: keep the panel but label the reconstruction caveat, corroborate with Trends, and let true snapshot-based momentum replace it as history accrues.
- How to earn trust: investors prioritize historical back-testing against known outcomes (Celsius/Bang/Alani), while the technical founder and category buyer prioritize calibration against current published market shares. These are complementary, not conflicting — both are low effort and both were bundled into one priority.
- What the tool is for today: the founders would already use it for positioning/flavor/messaging decisions; the growth investor and category buyer would not put it in front of a buyer or weight it in a dollar decision until velocity and c-store coverage exist. This is a scope disagreement, not a roadmap one — resolved by the coverage-note quick win, which makes the tool honest about which questions it can currently answer.
- Uniquely raised, not consensus: repeat/switching-language mining as a loyalty/churn proxy (growth investor only) and audience/demographic segmentation (technical founder only) — both worthwhile but deferred below the consensus items.

- Engineering discipline that makes the data trustworthy to regenerate: incremental merge/dedupe on natural keys, byte-identical CSV headers, offline fixture tests per scraper, and a single ~20KB aggregate so the dashboard can never silently diverge from the pipeline.
- Triangulation across demand signals most free tools skip: retail reviews + social engagement + Meta Ad Library paid messaging + Mintel/Statista anchors is the right skeleton — it mirrors how a real insights team layers syndicated + social + e-comm.
- Privacy posture (Reddit aggregates only, no usernames, raw data uncommitted) means the outputs are actually shareable/publishable, which most scraped datasets are not.

**Gaps:**
- No convenience-channel coverage. C-stores (7-Eleven, Circle K, gas) are the dominant energy-drink channel and drive single-can impulse pricing; Amazon is a tiny, pack-skewed, unrepresentative sliver. Any 'who is winning, where' answer from this data alone is structurally wrong about 'where'.
- Snapshot masquerading as time series. The 60-month t12m mention-share momentum is built from one crawl's upload dates, so it inherits YouTube search-ranking survivorship bias (deleted/demoted videos vanish, currently-popular old videos over-sample). Rising/cooling leaderboards need repeated dated snapshots, not retrospective reconstruction.
- Price comparisons are unnormalized. brand_summary.csv mixes 12-pack cases ($30.74 Monster), powder tubs ($49.50 AdvoCare), and singles into one 'avg price', so the price-x-quality scatter compares incomparable units; everything must be per-oz or per-serving.
- Tiny, uneven n with no uncertainty surfaced: exactly 15 Instagram posts per covered brand (a scrape-depth artifact presented as engagement share), ~24 reviews/brand, and 8 of 23 brands missing whole platforms — the dashboard shows point estimates with no min-n badges or confidence cues.
- No demand-side or demographic layer: no search-volume data, no audience segmentation (Celsius=female/fitness, Bang=gym, Monster=blue-collar/gaming — the category is demo-defined), and stage 5 'find white space' has no actual framework (no need-state mapping, no price-pack architecture, no TURF-style flavor-portfolio logic).

### Former national-retail category buyer turned CPG advisor (energy/beverage focus)

**Take:** This is a credible "why" layer — review text, theme sentiment, ad messaging, social momentum — which is exactly what Nielsen/Circana/SPINS don't give you, and the methodology hygiene (fractional attribution, partial-month exclusion, tested incremental scrapers) is better than most scrappy tools. But as it stands it cannot answer "who is winning, where" because it has no velocity, no distribution measure, and no convenience-store coverage, and energy is a c-store/DSD category where 55-60% of volume moves through channels this tool doesn't see. Useful today for positioning and messaging work on Bogus Banana; not yet something I'd put in front of a buyer or an investor without syndicated anchors.

**Strengths noted:**
- The full review-text corpus with theme-level sentiment (taste/crash/sugar/price/health) across brands is the genuine differentiator — syndicated data tells you what sold, never why, and buyers pay for exactly this qualitative layer.
- Methodology discipline is real: fractional attribution so share-of-voice sums to 100%, a music/entertainment noise filter, trailing-12-month momentum that drops the partial scrape month, and offline fixture tests per scraper — most homegrown tools skip all of this.
- Meta Ad Library capture (copy, CTA, campaign dates) is messaging intel that even Nielsen buyers rarely have systematized; paired with sentiment it can show claim-vs-complaint gaps.
- One ~20KB decision-ready aggregate feeding a single dashboard is the right architecture — cheap to rerun, easy to extend, no BI-tool tax.

**Gaps:**
- No sales/velocity signal at all — ratings, mentions, and engagement without units or dollars can't rank winners; Celsius looked mid on social long after it was the fastest-growing brand in track, and Bang looked huge on YouTube while it was collapsing at shelf.
- No convenience channel or DSD reality — energy is majority c-store single-serve, and the retailer set (Amazon, grocery, club) is the minority of category volume; Amazon in particular is structurally unrepresentative (multipacks, gift-y SKUs, 4.5+ rating J-curve).
- Sample sizes are decision-hostile and unflagged: brand_summary.csv shows brands rated on 8 Amazon reviews and Instagram fixed at 15 posts/brand — the dashboard presents these as comparable brand metrics with no n or confidence indicator.
- Momentum is built on YouTube mention share alone, which tracks media cycles (a Prime controversy, a Logan Paul video) rather than consumer demand — it needs corroboration from a retail-side signal before anyone acts on a 'riser'.
- Price is not normalized (per-oz/per-serving, pack size split) and there's no promo-depth/frequency tracking over time, so the price×quality panel mixes a $2.50 single can against a $30 12-pack — meaningless to a buyer; nutrition/ingredient capture (caffeine mg, sugar, electrolytes) is still roadmap, yet it's the axis the whole banana-electrolyte white-space claim depends on.
