WHERE THE NEW DEMAND GOES

The market adds roughly $11.7B between 2025 and 2030. This is that growth alone — not the market, just the increase — split by who captures it. It is the single most decision-relevant chart on the site.

    WHAT THIS CHART SAYS

    Women take about half of all new demand — more than five billion dollars, from an audience that barely existed in this category six years ago. Young adults take roughly another third, but they need $17.5B of existing base to do it, growing 4.5% a year against the women's segment's 17.2%.

    Read the small segments by growth rate rather than by size. Gamers & creators and Coffee drinkers contribute little in absolute dollars but compound fastest, and both are measured badly here because they sell through e-commerce and grocery channels this data barely covers. They are the segments most likely to be larger than shown.

    Calorie-cutters is the only audience that shrinks, and it is not a real collapse — sugar-free stopped being a distinct proposition once 72% of launches claimed it. Those sales did not disappear; they moved inside the other audiences.

    HOW FIRM ARE THESE NUMBERS

    The 2030 total is Mintel's central forecast, not mine. Its own 90% confidence band spans — — so treat the market total as a range, and the per-audience dollars as scaling with it.

    What I projected is the split, by extrapolating each audience's measured share drift (PDI 2021–2025, Mintel MULO 2025–2026) and damping it 15% a year so trends flatten instead of running away. Two figures are set by hand rather than trended — Gamers & creators and Health-conscious adults — because the channels they sell through are not visible in this data, and trending them from blind measurements would have driven them to zero.

    The women's line carries the result. It rests on a six-year trend that has not yet slowed. If Celsius and Alani Nu are nearer saturation than they look, that segment lands closer to 22% than 28.3%, and the young-adult decline is correspondingly gentler.
