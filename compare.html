    WHAT THIS CHART SAYS

    Women and young adults split the growth almost exactly evenly — about 43% each, roughly $5.1B apiece. But they get there in opposite ways: young adults need a $19.7B base growing 4.7% a year, while women compound at 19.4% from $3.6B. An audience that was 0% of the category in 2020 now matches the incumbent giants for absolute growth.

    Read the small segments by growth rate rather than by size. Gamers & creators and Coffee drinkers contribute little in absolute dollars but compound fastest, and both are measured badly here because they sell through e-commerce and grocery channels this data barely covers. They are the segments most likely to be larger than shown.

    Shift workers & military is the only audience that shrinks, and it is the clearest casualty of the channel shift: value brands are a convenience phenomenon, and convenience is the slowest-growing channel in the category. Passport has the segment falling from 5.5% of all-channel value in 2016 to 1.6% in 2025.

    HOW FIRM ARE THESE NUMBERS

    The 2030 total is Mintel's central forecast, not mine. Its own 90% confidence band spans — — so treat the market total as a range, and the per-audience dollars as scaling with it.

    What I projected is the split, by extrapolating each audience's measured share drift (Euromonitor Passport 2021–2025, all channels) and damping it 15% a year so trends flatten instead of running away. Two figures are set by hand rather than trended — Gamers & creators and Health-conscious adults — because the channels they sell through are not visible in this data, and trending them from blind measurements would have driven them to zero.

    The women's line carries the result, and it is already decelerating. Annual share gains ran +2.4, +4.1, +2.9 and then +1.0 points in 2025. The projection needs 1.84 points a year to reach 22.4%, so it is asking for 1.8× the most recent measured year. Treat 22.4% as the optimistic end; if the slowdown holds, this segment lands nearer 17%.
