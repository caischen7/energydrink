    The most important distinction on this page. Both look like gaps in a spreadsheet. Only one of them is.

    Genuinely untried

    Three SKUs or fewer have ever existed here, yet the flavor sells well to other audiences. Nobody has run the experiment.

      Tried and rejected

      Eight or more SKUs launched and the cell still earns under $0.5M. This is not white space — it is a graveyard, and a naive gap analysis would send you straight into it.

        CROSS-CHECK — WHAT CONSUMERS SAY THEY WANT

        Sales data shows what people bought from what was offered. This shows what they say they would buy if offered. Where the two agree, confidence is higher. Mintel 2026, n=766 US energy-drink consumers.

        Note the mismatch worth acting on: 39.4% want natural sweeteners and almost nothing on the shelf delivers it, while sugar-free — which 72% of recent launches now claim — has stopped being a differentiator at all.

        E-COMMERCE SIGNAL — A THIRD, INDEPENDENT SOURCE

        Neither PDI nor Mintel MULO can see inside e-commerce, which is the fastest-growing part of the market. The Amazon scrape is far too narrow to measure share, but it answers a different and still useful question: what exists online that never reaches a convenience cooler.

        — brands on Amazon are absent from the convenience data

        Note the pattern in the formats: powders, stick packs and hydration-adjacent products. E-commerce is not selling the same category in a different place — it is selling a different category.

          Claims appearing in Amazon listing copy

          Mentions across every listing scraped. The bottom row is the finding, and it corroborates the survey from a completely independent direction.

            Zero mentions of natural sweeteners across every listing, against 39.4% of surveyed consumers saying they want exactly that. Two unrelated sources — stated preference and online assortment — pointing at the same hole is the strongest signal on this page.

            The category assumes it does. Tested against 68,930 YouTube titles and 150,514 comments on one side and PDI revenue on the other, using the same flavor taxonomy for both — it does not. Reported here because a null result on this question changes how every other social signal on this site should be read.

            LAUNCH SIMULATOR — WHAT RANGE OF OUTCOMES TO EXPECT

            Everything above describes what already happened. This runs the forward question 10,000 times: pick a pocket, and the simulator resamples the complete revenue history of real launches into it. It is a bootstrap, not a model — nothing is fitted, so the skew and the failure rate come from the data rather than from an assumption about its shape.
